Japan's immigration policies are set to tighten like a vice on a fragile balloon.
Japanese government officials push for stricter immigration controls
The move aims to reduce competition for native workers in a stagnant economy
Immigration would be limited to exceptional cases based on national interests
Critics argue the policy would stifle innovation and hinder economic growth
Proponents say the measures are necessary to protect Japanese culture and identity
The plan faces opposition from foreign worker advocacy groups and human rights activists
