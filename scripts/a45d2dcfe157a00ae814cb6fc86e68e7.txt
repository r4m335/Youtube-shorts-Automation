You won't believe the weird unit that's part of the metric system but never gets used in real life.
The Are unit was defined in 1972, equal to 100 square meters.
It's a relic from a failed attempt to standardize surface area measurements.
Despite being part of the International System of Units, it's hardly ever referenced in science or daily life.
In some Eastern European countries, it still shows up in official documents, but even then, it's mostly symbolic.
It's a mystery why it survived in the metric system so long.
